Behaviour designSofa (self)repair
My role

Sr. Product UX Designer at IKEA · 2026 - 2027

The brief

10.5m tonnes of furniture ends in landfill yearly. Sofas are complex in material and take up a lot of space. Circularity is an important topic at IKEA, therefore the goal was to reduce (bulky) waste on landfills and encourage repair - but how to change this behaviour?

The goal

In case of damage, households will repair their sofa and extend its life to keep it off of landfills for as long as possible.

The process

For this project I followed a behaviour science process and techniques. I went into literature, recruited and interviewed people in the UK with broken (and repaired or disposed) sofas and learned about their repair motivations and barriers.

The challenge

To set myself up for success, I needed to get to know the right people. As a designer not positioned in the sustainability team, I needed to rely on others quite a bit. Involving them early on and particularly when moving from theory to practice, helped me move things forward.

Behaviour designCollections takeover
My role

Sr. Product UX Designer at IKEA · 2026 - 2027

The brief

Increase commerciality

The goal

To inform visitors and create visibility on IKEA.com when a new collection is coming or has arrived.

The process

This project was in collaboration with another designer. We mapped the pages where we envisioned the collection information to go for three different moments (a week before, the day before and during). I focussed on the 'during' and wanted to amplify the characteristics of the collection (IKEA PS 202). I did so by highlighting three products from this collection and making them playfully interactive.

The challenge

An end-to-end journey like this had never been done before. We had to convince stakeholders to go take this approach and celebrate our new collections. Because there was no process in place and we aspired to make this project scallable, we had to swim against the stream for a fair amount of time.
